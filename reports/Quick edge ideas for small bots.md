# Rest maker orders; stop hunting taker edges

**The honest answer is that almost nothing a $200–$500 bot can do will *prove* an edge within 1–3 months. The one candidate with academic support is passive liquidity provision: resting maker orders on favourites in Kalshi series where makers pay no fee.** Everything else is either already arbitraged by professionals, killed by fees at this size, or too slow to test forward in that window. Kalshi's best study found that takers lost **−31.5%** on average after fees and makers **−9.6%**, while makers buying contracts at 50¢ or more earned **+2.6%** ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Our own K1 weather test found the same shape: a 1–1.5¢ favourite–longshot overpricing, taker rules at −0.44¢ per contract, and a maker upper bound of +1.5–2.5¢. Even if that edge is real, it is worth single-digit to low-double-digit dollars a month on this bankroll. It also needs on the order of a thousand fills to separate from noise, so the 1–3-month window is a test of fills and adverse selection rather than proof of profit. Stock and crypto ideas fare worse. Published anomalies shrink by about **58% after publication** ([McLean & Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)), and our R1–R4 backtests have already shown what that decay looks like. Low-tier crypto fees run **0.5–2.4% per round trip**, and cross-venue arbitrage on regulated US venues needs gaps above about **3.3¢** just to break even. The base rate is harsh: **82–84% of Polymarket wallets lose** ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/prediction-markets-vs-stock-market-returns/)), and fewer than **1% of Taiwanese day traders** earn reliable profits net of fees ([Barber et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v18y2014icp1-24.html)).

## Fees decide every Kalshi idea before the forecast does

Kalshi's July 2026 schedule charges takers `roundup(0.07 × C × P × (1−P))`. That is at most **1.75¢ per contract at 50¢**, and as a share of stake about **6.3% at 10¢, 3.5% at 50¢ and 0.7% at 90¢**. Makers pay `roundup(0.0175 × C × P × (1−P))` only in series that carry a maker multiplier, and **the default multiplier is 0** ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/fee-schedule)). The series that charge makers are economics (CPI, Fed, payrolls, U3, GDP), monthly gas, yearly index ranges, and most sports including ATP/WTA matches. Weather (highs, lows, rain, snow), TSA, and daily and weekly gas are not on the list. On our reading of the schedule, **maker orders in those series are free**. Treat that as an assumption until the API's per-series `fee_multiplier` field confirms it. Per-order rounding is a hidden tax at this size: one contract at 5¢ pays a 1¢ fee, which is **20%** of the stake. A small bot must trade in lots of 10–100 or rest orders.

Bürgi, Deng & Whelan's dataset covers **313,972 contract observations from 2021 to April 2025**. It shows a favourite–longshot bias that is significant in every category. **Contracts at 10¢ or below lose over 60%**, and contracts above 70¢ earn small but statistically significant positive post-fee returns ([Bürgi et al.](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). Their category regressions matter for picking where to look. **Climate & Weather has one of the mildest longshot penalties** (intercept −1.0, implied break-even around 32¢). Economics is the closest to efficient, with an insignificant intercept. Entertainment (−2.8) and Politics (−1.9) are the worst places to buy cheap contracts, and neither shows a reliable favourite premium ([Bürgi et al., Table 8](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). There are two caveats. First, the +2.6% is an average per contract snapshot from before maker fees, not a backtest of a bot that has to win its fills. Second, the bias may be fading: the price slope fell from **0.048 in 2024 to 0.021 in Jan–Apr 2025**, significant only at p < 0.1 ([Bürgi et al., Table 9](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). K1 independently hinted at shrinkage, so two separate datasets point the same way. Any maker strategy should be judged on the most recent months of the tape, not the full history.

Public weather backtests agree with K1 on the taker side. A walk-forward test of daily highs concluded that free weather data cannot beat Kalshi. Its only profitable results were maker results that assumed every order filled ([GitHub backtest](https://github.com/MiguelTheGamer/kalshi-weather-backtest)). A 1,506-event study found no strategy consistently profitable after fees ([oddsreference](https://oddsreference.com/predictions/research/kalshi-weather-model)). We found **no credible public evidence of a profitable systematic retail Kalshi strategy after fees**. That is why K2, which simulates maker fills on the real trade tape, is the single most important experiment in this project.

## Kalshi categories rank as: lows and TSA maybe, the rest no

Settled-market volumes pulled from Kalshi's public API on 2026-11-24 show where a small bot can even fit ([Kalshi API](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?series_ticker=KXLOWTNYC&status=settled)):

| Category | Volume (contracts per event, median) | Maker fee | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily LOW temperature (KXLOWTNYC / CHI) | ~31k NYC, ~13k CHI (vs ~162k for the NYC high) | 0 | **Test**: same pipeline and settlement family as highs |
| TSA weekly (KXTSAW) / daily (KXTSAD) | ~12.7k / ~3k | 0 | **Maybe**: real information structure, tiny depth |
| Daily / weekly AAA gas | ~417k / ~2.0M | 0 | **Maybe (low)**: liquid but likely a speed game |
| Rain, snow, hourly temperature | Sporadic; hourly and snow showed no settled markets | 0 | Skip |
| CPI, payrolls, U3, Fed | 0.35M–103M | Charged | Skip |
| Mentions, Billboard/Spotify, box office | One-off series | Varies | Skip |

**Daily lows** are the cheapest new category to test. NYC trades about 31k contracts a day across 6 strikes. The Weather Company settles the KXLOWT series ([Kalshi series metadata](https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/series?category=Climate%20and%20Weather)), and the existing NBM pipeline transfers directly. Five cities give about 150 settled events a month. Whether lows are harder or easier to price than highs is unknown, and nobody has published a mispricing study of them.

**TSA** has the best information structure on the list but the worst depth. KXTSAW settles on the Monday–Sunday average of TSA screenings ([CFTC rule filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules0426231013.pdf)). TSA posts on weekdays, so by Friday a bot knows Monday through Thursday, and only three highly seasonal days remain. However, only **1–3 of 21 strikes sit between 10¢ and 90¢** in a typical week. One builder reported doing "surprisingly well" but estimated he "could probably make $20/week" and found it hard to buy more than a handful of contracts ([Ferraiolo](https://ferraijv.github.io/kalshi_tsa_trading_bot_overview/)). With 52 weekly events a year, three months yields only about 13 events. The weekly market cannot produce evidence inside the window, and the daily KXTSAD is even thinner.

**Gas** is deeply liquid, but AAA publishes the settlement number every morning and it moves slowly. The likely mispricings are stale quotes that fast players clear. **Economics** markets are where professionals price against futures and nowcasts. Makers pay fees there, and Bürgi et al. find near-efficiency. **Mentions and entertainment** are one-off events that no systematic model can learn from, and they carry the worst longshot penalties in the data.

## Other venues help only as a monitor, not as a trade

A US bot has three realistic API venues: Kalshi, **Polymarket US** (CFTC-regulated, Retail API since February 2026), and **ForecastEx** through the IBKR TWS API. Polymarket US's taker charge is about **0.06 × p(1−p)**, roughly 1.5¢ at 50¢. Since 19 October 2026 its maker rebate requires **$10M of monthly maker volume**, which is zero for a small bot ([Polymarket US changelog](https://docs.polymarket.us/changelog); [AgentBets](https://agentbets.ai/guides/polymarket-us-api-guide/)). Sources conflict on category-specific fee rates, so the official fee page must be read before modelling. A temporary 70% API taker rebate runs only until 31 December 2026 ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2307128)). ForecastEx is the cheapest leg at **$0.01 per contract per side** ([IBKR](https://brokerage.ibkr.com/en/pricing/commissions-events.php?re=amer)). It is buy-only and leans toward economics and politics, so it overlaps little with Kalshi. **PredictIt bans automated trading** and charges 10% of profit plus 5% on withdrawals ([Alphascope](https://www.alphascope.app/blog/predictit-api); [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/prediction-markets/predictit-review/)). Robinhood and Coinbase mostly route to Kalshi and offer no retail event API.

The academic evidence on cross-venue gaps is real but misleading for a small bot. Gebele & Matthes studied 102,275 events and found **only about 2% of Kalshi markets have a counterpart elsewhere**, with median deviations of **2–4%** from execution-adjusted parity. Their headline hold-to-resolution return of 1,218% came from **just 15 trades** ([arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01706v1)). Gaps persist mainly because the contracts are not truly identical (different settlement wording and sources) and because capital is locked up. Fast sports gaps are a different regime. One builder logged 870 Kalshi–Polymarket gaps in 24 hours with a **median life of about 9 seconds**, and netted **$439 over an entire World Cup match** ([DEV](https://dev.to/michaelmustopo/built-a-prediction-market-arbitrage-no-sizable-arbitrage-found-36cf)).

Our arithmetic at mid prices shows why this fails at our size. Kalshi taker (~1.75–2¢) plus Polymarket US taker (~1.5¢) sets a break-even gap of about **3.3¢**. A 5¢ gap nets about 1.6¢ on 95¢ of capital that stays locked until resolution. With $150 per leg, that is roughly **$2.40 per opportunity at best**. One settlement mismatch that loses both legs erases about 60 good trades. The two venues also have no published 2026 measurement of how often their gaps exceed 3.3¢. The only honest next step is a read-only logger.

## Stock and crypto ideas mostly repeat what R1–R4 already showed

Our own results set the baseline. Momentum with low volatility (R1) and momentum with a crash filter (R2) failed. Intraday SPY momentum (R3) showed **48.2% sign agreement and −14%/yr net**, even though the published effect was real on 1993–2013 data at about **1.4% out-of-sample R²** ([Gao et al.](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). The literature explains the gap. Baltussen et al. tie the effect to gamma hedging by option market makers ([JFE 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:142:y:2021:i:1:p:377-403)), which makes it regime-dependent rather than constant. Insider purchases (R4) are the most instructive failure. The signal is genuine: **opportunistic insider trades earned 82 bp/month** in Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454)), and R4 reached Sharpe 1.06 at 5 bps. But the return lives in small caps, and R4 turned its portfolio over **194 times a year**. At 15 bps it fell to Sharpe 0.35 and at 30 bps to $21, below SPY's 0.80. The one untested lever is to cut turnover and trade only liquid names, using the CMP opportunistic filter and cluster buys. Cluster buys earn about **3.8% over 21 days versus 2.0%** for single purchases ([IBKR Campus summary](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/stocks/what-corporate-insider-buying-can-tell-investors-evidence-from-academic-research/)). That variant can be backtested immediately on data already parsed.

The rest of the stock list fails on evidence:

| Idea | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| Overnight returns | Real gross pattern, but costs erase most or all of it and it weakened after 2009 ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/); [Elm Wealth](https://elmwealth.com/night-shift/)) |
| Post-earnings drift | Gone for large caps by about 2006 ([Martineau, CFR 2022](https://nowpublishers.com/article/Details/CFR-0122)), though contested ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again)). What survives sits in microcaps with wide spreads |
| S&P index additions | Abnormal return fell from **7.4% in the 1990s to under 1%** ([Greenwood & Sammon, JF 2025](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4294297)) |
| Turn-of-month and pre-holiday | Turn-of-month gone over 2006–2023 in one study ([UCP thesis](https://repositorio.ucp.pt/bitstreams/8a4ad0ac-a69c-42d2-8611-9a7149577751/download)); pre-holiday effect much weaker after 1987 ([core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/works/2843318)) |
| Dividend capture | Prices drop by about 0.8 of the dividend ([NBER w0496](https://www.nber.org/papers/w0496)); short holds are taxed as ordinary income |
| Sector-ETF momentum | Robust historically ([core.ac.uk](https://core.ac.uk/download/363989861.pdf)), but a monthly rebalance needs a decade to judge, and R1/R2 already sampled this family |

The arithmetic of noise rules out forward tests for all of these. Assume a 3 bp intraday edge against 25 bp of noise. Reaching t ≈ 2 takes about 280 trades, which is more than a year.

Crypto spot starts deeper in the hole. Lowest-tier round trips cost **0.50% taker on Alpaca** ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/crypto-fees)), **1.6% on Kraken Pro** ([Kraken](https://www.kraken.com/pro/fees)), **2.4% on Gemini** ([Gemini](https://www.gemini.com/fees/activetrader-fee-schedule)), and a reported 1.8% on Coinbase US after September 2026 ([CryptoDaily](https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/09/coinbase-advanced-fees-usdc-vip-tiers)). A $500 account never leaves the lowest tier. In-sample bitcoin time-series momentum is strong ([Liu & Tsyvinski](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24877)). But intraday technical rules fall from **Sharpe 0.66 in-sample to 0.06 out of sample** and do not survive costs ([Shen et al.](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/100181/3/21Sep2021Bitcoin%20Intraday%20Time-Series%20Momentum.R2.pdf)). Altcoin momentum disappears once delisted coins are included ([Liebi survivorship paper](https://www.sebastianstoeckl.com/publications/wp2022_liebi_survivorship/)). Cross-exchange spreads are large mainly across countries with capital controls, not within the US ([Makarov & Schoar](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/130495/SSRN-id3171204.pdf)). Funding-rate carry needs regulated perps. Coinbase's nano BTC-PERP is 0.01 BTC per contract ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/363796/coinbase-launches-perpetual-futures)), which likely exceeds the account once margin is added. A weekly BTC/ETH trend filter on Alpaca is the only crypto variant worth a backtest. It is a cousin of the crash filter that already failed in R2, and the 35% worst-drop bar will be its binding constraint.

Among the other ideas, **tennis** stays research-only. Bookmaker consensus was the best of 11 forecasters ([Kovalchik 2016](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bpj:jqsprt:v:12:y:2016:i:3:p:127-138:n:1)), and ML betting returns were mostly negative ([Wilkens 2021](https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4073773?lng=en)). The lower tiers, where mispricing is likeliest, are also where **464 players reported first-hand knowledge of match-fixing** ([ITIA IRP report](https://itia.tennis/media/bjuateer/irp-report-final.pdf)), so apparent edges may be informed flow from fixers. Kalshi tennis series also charge maker fees ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-fee-schedule-by-market)). **Matched betting** breaks sportsbook terms when it uses duplicate accounts, and books limit customers for bonus abuse ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/sports-betting/story/_/id/41231266/espn-sports-betting-news-sportsbooks-defend-practice-limiting-sharp-customers)), so it cannot be a bot. **CME weather futures** cost $20 per HDD index point ([CME](https://cmegroup.com/markets/weather/temperature/us-monthly-weather-heating.contractSpecs.html)). A roughly 900-point January contract is about $18,000 of notional, far beyond this account.

## The five tests worth running, and what to drop

The ranking weighs evidence of a real effect, time to a verdict, and reuse of existing code. For Kalshi strategies, "beat SPY" means converting daily P&L to an annualised Sharpe above **0.80** with a worst drop under 35%, on a held-out period. The per-contract test must also clear zero with a confidence interval. One honest piece of arithmetic sets expectations. A favourite at 80¢ has a per-fill standard deviation of about 40¢. Detecting a 2¢ edge at t ≈ 2 therefore needs about **(2 × 40 / 2)² ≈ 1,600 fills**, and fills inside the same event are correlated. The historical tape must carry the statistical proof. Forward paper trading checks fill rates and adverse selection.

| Rank | Idea | Data | Rule | Pass bar (after costs) | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Maker on favourites, fee-free weather series (finish K2)** | Kalshi trade tape plus order snapshots for KXHIGH*/KXLOWT* | Rest a bid 1–2¢ inside the touch on strikes priced 70–95¢; count a fill only when the tape trades through the bid | Net ≥ +1¢ per filled contract on the most recent 3 months held out, CI above 0, and Sharpe > 0.80 | K2 on history now, plus 6–8 weeks of forward paper to compare simulated against real fill rates |
| 2 | **Daily LOW temperature** | NBM forecasts plus KXLOWT* prices for 5 cities | Rank-1 model with the K1 harness, maker only | Model Brier/log-loss better than the market mid at entry time over ≥300 events, and positive maker P&L | 4–8 weeks (about 150 events a month) |
| 3 | **Insider buys v2 (low turnover, liquid)** | Existing R4 SEC Form 4 parse | Opportunistic (CMP) or cluster buys only, entry at the next open after filing, market cap above a liquidity floor, hold 21 days | Sharpe > SPY at **30 bps per side**, worst drop < 35%, trained on 2017–2022 and tested on 2023–2026 | Backtest in days |
| 4 | **TSA weekly/daily nowcast maker** | TSA daily throughput (free) plus KXTSAW/KXTSAD books | Same weekday last year × YoY trend projection; from Thursday on, rest maker orders at the decisive strike | Model log-loss better than the market on Friday/Saturday snapshots, and positive maker P&L | 3–6 months; slowest of the five |
| 5 | **Cross-venue gap logger (no trading)** | Kalshi + Polymarket US (+ ForecastEx) quotes on hand-verified identical contracts | Log the fee-adjusted gap after both taker fees, using full fees rather than the expiring rebate | Gaps above 3.3¢ that last over 5 minutes and sum to more than about $10/week of capturable profit at $150 per leg | 4 weeks |

Skip these: taker strategies on any Kalshi category (−31% average; K1 −0.44¢), longshots at 10¢ or below, economics, mentions and entertainment markets, rain/snow/hourly weather, fast sports cross-venue arbitrage, PredictIt and Polymarket International, intraday or daily crypto trading, altcoin momentum, US crypto exchange arbitrage, overnight-only SPY, turn-of-month and holiday timing, PEAD on microcaps, index-addition trades, dividend capture, options, matched betting, CME weather futures, and any live tennis trading. Daily and weekly gas sit on the line. Test them only by reusing the rank-1 harness, since the edge there is probably speed.

## Conclusion

The base rate over 1–3 months is worse than it looks. Over 300 days, **97% of Brazilian persistent day traders lost money** ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). Fewer than 1% of Taiwanese day traders win reliably, and the top 1% of Polymarket winners capture **about 85% of profits** ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/prediction-markets-vs-stock-market-returns/)). Over a short window, though, noise will put a sizeable minority of losing strategies in the black. Our rough estimate is 20–40%, which is an inference, not a measured figure. A profitable first month is therefore close to uninformative. Kill tests must be pre-registered and sized by the fill-count arithmetic above, not by the calendar.

The deeper lesson from R1–R4 and K1 is that **costs and turnover, not signal, are the binding constraint at $200–$500**. Insider buying had a real signal and still failed. Kalshi weather has a real bias, and takers still lose. The ideas that survive, maker-only and fee-free or low-turnover and liquid, survive because they avoid paying the spread rather than because they forecast better. The prize is small even if K2 passes: a few percent per contract on a few hundred dollars of depth is tens of dollars a month. The realistic payoff of the next three months is a verified fill model and a trustworthy harness. Those are worth having before the account is larger and before the favourite–longshot bias, which already shows signs of shrinking, is gone.
